# Three ways to move language

The board contains actual frames from original MIT engine studies. Read each row
left to right as a sequence; do not copy its words into the user's project.

1. **Type as a stage:** large lettering, moving bands and a strong framing change
   make the composition evolve. Adapt the scale hierarchy and directional handoff.
   Required copy needs an intact reading pose even if decorative type is cropped.
2. **Letter/form contrast:** the film changes size, tracking, background and type
   treatment between distinct beats. Use a coherent contrast, such as compact to
   open or quiet to bold. These are supported text properties, not variable axes.
3. **Stroke progression:** the handwriting study draws authored monoline paths.
   It demonstrates stroke direction and pacing, not a built-in ability to convert
   arbitrary text into handwriting. Use owned paths/Lottie when available;
   otherwise retain editable type and choose an honest text reveal.

Try a phrase as three poses: one emphasized word, its relationship with supporting
words, and the complete statement. Recompose for the actual wording and duration.
Preserve spaces and reading order when splitting text into nodes. A late reveal
must still leave time to read the result.

Sources: `kinetic`, `type-motion`, `handwriting`. Original films are twelve seconds;
sampled frames and source video hashes are recorded in `provenance.json`.
