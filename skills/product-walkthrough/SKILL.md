# Product walkthrough

Show what changes when someone uses the product. Study the attached
[reference board](references/atlas.jpg) and [motion notes](references/atlas.md)
for focus, causality and detail reveals. A walkthrough is a sequence the viewer
can follow, not a collection of decorative interface cards.

## Establish what is real

Use supplied steps, existing project facts and owned product assets. Preserve
button labels, feature names, UI order, claims and identity. If the user asks for
a real product demonstration but essential screens or actions are unavailable,
ask for that material instead of inventing features or plausible-looking results.
A product URL alone does not enable an unavailable browsing or asset-fetch tool.

If the user explicitly asks for a concept interface or fictional demo, compose
one and label it as a concept. A drawn cursor illustrates an action; it does not
click a real application. Never tell the user that the demonstrated invite,
purchase, upload or other external action has actually happened.

Choose a compact sequence: establish the relevant state, show a specific action,
show its visible response, then hold the supplied result or takeaway. The benefit
must follow from the supplied workflow. Do not invent performance, revenue or
customer claims. A physical product can reveal known parts or a supplied mechanism
without asserting unprovided engineering, nutrition or health properties.

## Direct attention through the workflow

Anchor the interface/product before focusing a detail. Move related parts
together, preserve their arrangement, and leave enough time to recognize each
state. Let a cursor, focus ring, highlight or object movement lead to the response;
feedback must follow the action rather than precede it.

For screenshots, crop/pan deliberately while preserving aspect ratio and
legibility. For a rebuilt concept UI, keep text, cards and controls as editable
native elements with stable IDs. A simplified interface should clarify the
supplied action, not impersonate an unseen current product screen.

Use one coherent visual language for spacing, corners, typography and accents.
Avoid filling empty space with badges or invented notifications. Reuse elements
across state changes so the relationship stays clear. Reserve a clean final hold
rather than continuous cursor movement after the result is established.

## Author within the existing project

Keep unrelated items and global format settings. Put the demo's backdrop inside
its own graphic. Use installed grammar, local child clocks and plain positioned
groups for absolute layouts. Screenshots remain owned assets; do not fabricate
asset IDs, URLs or browser interactions.

Check exact labels and the requested state sequence in `review_edit`, including
placement and duration. A review receipt does not prove a real product action or
claim. Finalize the exact successful candidate with `propose_edit({reviewed:true})`.
