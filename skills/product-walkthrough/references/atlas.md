# Show an action and its consequence

Rows are original concept studies, not evidence about the user's actual product.
Borrow their visual relationships while keeping supplied features and claims exact.

1. **Action and response:** cards become an organized workspace, attention moves
   to an action, and the final state resolves. The viewer can connect the changed
   arrangement to the workflow. Preserve that causality for the user's known steps.
2. **Layers and detail:** an assembly exposes parts, then reunites them. Use
   separation to explain a supplied structure or an explicitly fictional concept.
   Do not invent internal components for a real product. For software, a related
   idea is to separate interface layers, focus one, then restore the whole view.
3. **Product cause and effect:** the physical-product study follows ingredients,
   pouring and the finished presentation. Motion connects the subjects instead
   of merely switching slogans. The sample's brand and details are not new facts.

A short walkthrough often needs fewer states than a tutorial. For a longer flow,
preserve all requested steps and plan connected scenes rather than accelerating
illegible screens. Simplify presentation, not the user's facts. Distinguish an
illustrative cursor from a real action receipt.

recorded in `provenance.json`.
