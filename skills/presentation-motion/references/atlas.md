# Motion follows the argument

Each row shows three moments from an original engine study, not a slide template
or source of business facts. Sample statistics and company names are illustrative.

1. **One idea per beat:** the company study reuses a palette and sculptural motif
   across chapters. The motif hands attention to the next title. A presentation
   can reuse a diagram element or alignment anchor without repeating a full layout.
2. **Editorial hierarchy:** the data study separates title, evidence and supported
   emphasis. Establish the reading target before secondary annotations. Use
   chart-storytelling for actual numeric encoding; never borrow sample values.
3. **Detail and context:** the software study establishes a workspace, focuses on
   one action and returns to its result. A training slide can isolate a component,
   explain it, then return to the overall diagram.

For an ordered process, preserve order in timing and spatial placement. Keep
connectors attached to moving targets. For a comparison, establish both sides
before highlighting the supported distinction. For a title slide, one precise
transformation and a clear reading hold can be enough. Choose from the content,
not from the board's row order.

Sources: `company`, `data-story`, `software`; see `provenance.json` for frame and
video hashes. These examples demonstrate video motion, not PPTX editing support.
