# Read this board as choreography

Rows are three different design directions; columns are successive moments of
each original 12-second film. Adapt the relationships to the current duration,
format and occasion. These are visual studies, not templates or project assets.

1. **Type as stage:** monumental lettering and diagonal color fields make the
   whole frame participate. One word/number becomes a stage; the next beat changes
   the composition before the identity settles. Borrow the scale contrast and
   directional handoff, not the sample words or checkerboard everywhere.
2. **Sculptural celebration:** a dimensional centerpiece, orbit and typography
   share one visual world. Depth, lighting and foreground/background separation
   do more than a larger particle count. For a six-second birthday, a wrapped
   object could open into the age, with ribbons carrying the reveal into the name.
3. **Organic reveal:** overlapping vector forms unfurl in waves, then open a calm
   reading area. Build two or three related shapes with stagger and layered depth;
   quantity is not the idea. A formal anniversary could reveal a precise emblem
   through measured arcs rather than borrowing birthday balloons.

Choose the direction because it fits the brief. A concise concept might be
“paper strips unfold into a birthday stage, then sweep aside to reveal Maya.”
Describe the visual action before choosing a palette. A single large disc, age,
name and scattered confetti is only a starting card, not a finished motion story.

## Translate the reference into authored beats

For a roughly six-second graphic, a useful starting rhythm is: anticipation in
the first half-second; a distinctive transformation over the next two seconds;
the name/message becomes the focus; then at least 1.5 seconds of clear final hold.
Use that rhythm as a design aid, not a mandatory duration formula. Make at least
two adjacent beats visibly different in silhouette, arrangement or framing;
changing opacity alone does not create a new composition.

Use native groups/shapes for paper, masks and type, SVG import for a bespoke motif,
or the installed Three scene grammar for a genuinely dimensional centerpiece.
Keep exact names and ages as whole editable text nodes. Never invent a logo, date,
venue or cultural symbol from a person's name. The board's copy is illustrative.
