# Type and color

Art-direct an editable title, motion poster, or visual identity: choose a type pairing, palette and focal movement from the brief. Use for original looks or a substantial restyle.

Start with what the viewer should notice and what should change. Name one visual event in plain words, such as “a folded flower opens to reveal the title.” Design a frame of real copy at its final size before decorating it. A preset name is not a concept. Preserve supplied branding, wording and unrelated edits.

## Three starting points

Use these as working combinations, not mandatory themes. Match the subject, the available footage and the intended mood. All named faces here are available in the default local editor for Latin text.

- **Moon garden · patient, organic.** Fraunces at 500–650 for a generous display line. IBM Plex Mono at 500 for a short supporting line. Plum-black ground `#21172E`, warm paper type `#F8EFDA`, violet material `#BBA3EC`, citron cue `#DDF583`. Leave an asymmetrical pocket of quiet around the type. Let a curve, petal or shadow unfold first; let the cue appear at the moment of recognition. Keep small lettering upright and bright.
- **Ink press · graphic, physical.** Anton at its real 400 weight for oversized compact words; Space Grotesk at 500–650 for supporting copy. Shell-pink stock `#F6C5BB`, near-black ink `#252035`, vermilion printed shapes `#BC3827`. Cut a silhouette into the page, slide two printed forms into register, then stop cleanly. Pink against red can be beautiful for large shapes; use ink on pink for small text. Do not fake multiple Anton weights.
- **Blue hour · clear, spacious.** Space Grotesk at 600 for the main statement; Lora at 400 for one human line. Ultramarine ground `#193DAD`, porcelain type `#FFF4DB`, pale aqua material `#BCE5D8`, apricot cue `#FFAF83`. Use alignment and a shared moving edge to connect two states. Reserve the warm accent for the one item the viewer should follow.

## Make the combination work

Use color as **ground, type, material and cue**. They need not be four different colors. Sample an existing shot when it matters. A cue earns attention by arriving on the action it identifies. A permanently highlighted destination can spoil the reveal. Never rely on color alone to communicate a number or selection.

Test the actual phrase, including accents and long words. For a 1280×720 title study, try a display line around 80–150 px and supporting copy around 24–36 px, then adapt to the canvas and actual glyphs. Preserve a readable size rather than shrinking a long title to fit an arbitrary column. Keep a common edge or baseline across roles. Avoid tracking body text as widely as a short uppercase label. Use the script-appropriate available Noto face when a Latin subset does not cover the text.

Build the gesture from native shape, transform, clip and opacity tracks; let the type and motion support the same idea. Stagger related parts, not everything on screen. Anticipation, a decisive change and a settled hold work at different speeds; a slow pose can be intentional. For a short title film give the complete message at least a moment of stillness. A beautiful end frame does not rescue an unreadable transition.

Read the opening, the most crowded moving frame and the final hold at thumbnail size when a preview is available. Check the level of contrast _over the actual backdrop_. Repair clipping, accidental tangencies, tiny copy, and accents that distract from the subject. Keep required words as editable native text and the artwork independently adjustable. Do not claim visual approval from structural validation alone.
